# Anti-library sample

Chapter 1  The Unread Shelf

A private library is not an ornament. The shelf of books you have not read is
the useful half of it: it is the part that still has something to tell you, and
the part that keeps the size of what you do not know in front of you. Paragraph 1.

Read books are behind you; unread ones are questions that have not been asked
yet. A library grows more valuable as the unread portion grows, which is the
opposite of how most people are taught to think about shelves. Paragraph 2.

The longer a paragraph runs, the more there is for a typesetter to do with it —
where to break, how far to stretch the spaces, whether tightening this line will
pay for itself two lines further down. Paragraph 3.

This one is here to be selected with the mouse, marked with `1` through `4`, and
copied with Ctrl+C, so that what comes back out of the clipboard can be compared
with what is on the page. Paragraph 4.

And this one is long enough to wrap several times in a narrow column, which is
what the measure setting changes. Drag the measure and watch the breaks move.
Paragraph 5.
